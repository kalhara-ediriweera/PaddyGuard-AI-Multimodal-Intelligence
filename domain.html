Section 1

Literature Survey

Critical analysis of state-of-the-art research across all 4 research components

🌿

1. Deep Learning for Rice Leaf Disease Classification

Mohanty et al. [6] achieved 99.35% accuracy using AlexNet on PlantVillage, while Lu et al. [7] designed a CNN achieving 94.7% specifically for rice diseases. Chen et al. [8] showed that EfficientNet architectures (EfficientNetB3/B4) achieve comparable accuracy to ResNet models with significantly fewer parameters. However, Ferentinos [9] highlighted that accuracy sharply drops on real field images, and none of these models incorporate Out-of-Distribution rejection mechanisms to handle non-rice foliage.

🦗

2. Deep Learning & YOLO for Rice Pest Detection

Pest infestations cause 20% to 40% yield loss in rice worldwide (Savary et al. [2]). Hu et al. [5] demonstrated that CNNs extract complex visual features of insect pests from agricultural imagery, outperforming traditional ML. Zheng et al. [3] adapted lightweight YOLOv8 models for real-time paddy insect detection. However, existing pest systems rely on closed-set training and misidentify unfamiliar non-pest insects as major pests, triggering unwarranted spraying.

🎙️

3. Speech Recognition & NLP in Agriculture

Smallholder farmers in South Asia often experience low digital literacy and lack technical botanical vocabulary. Devlin et al. [15] demonstrated bidirectional transformer representations (BERT) for text understanding. Prior agricultural advisory systems predominantly depend on text-based web forms in English. Developing end-to-end speech recognition (Whisper / Vosk) with domain symptom extraction in Sinhala represents an under-explored, high-impact research opportunity.

💊

4. RAG Chatbots & Out-of-Distribution Reliability

Lewis et al. [16] pioneered Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to ground LLMs in authoritative knowledge. In agriculture, unconstrained LLMs hallucinate illegal pesticides or toxic dosage levels. For reliability, Liang et al. [10] (ODIN), Lee et al. [11] (Mahalanobis Distance), and Bendale & Boult [12] (OpenMax) established mathematical frameworks to reject out-of-distribution inputs. Selvaraju et al. [13] introduced Grad-CAM for visual saliency verification.

Research Gap

Limitations of existing solutions versus the proposed PaddyGuard AI architecture

Evaluation Dimension Existing Agricultural Systems (Plantix, Rice Doctor, Paddy Doctor) PaddyGuard AI Multimodal Research System
Input Modalities Image-only or manual text questionnaires. Excludes low-literacy farmers. Multimodal: Ingests Sinhala spoken speech + foliar leaf images + field pest images.
Foliar Disease Scope (C2) Closed-world CNNs; forces non-rice leaves/weeds into disease classes with high false confidence. Open-Set Aware: Integrated OOD screening (ODIN, Mahalanobis, OpenMax) rejects non-rice inputs.
Pest Detection Scope (C4) Limited insect datasets; misidentifies benign insects as pests; no unknown insect rejection. YOLOv8 + OOD: Detects target pests while rejecting unfamiliar insect species.
Speech & Language (C1) English or closed-form menus. Lacks local phonetic support for rural dialects. Sinhala Voice Pipeline: Whisper/Vosk ASR + TF-IDF symptom keywords + Sinhala TTS speech response.
Treatment Guidance (C3) Generic text summaries or black-box LLMs prone to hallucinating toxic pesticide dosages. Hybrid RAG + Expert Rules: Grounded in IRRI/FAO manuals + deterministic safety barrier + acreage dosage calc.
Model Interpretability Black-box classification outputs without visual evidence. Grad-CAM Visual Heatmaps: Visually verifies that model attends to genuine pathological lesions.
Section 3

Research Problem

The multi-faceted challenges confronting smallholder paddy farmers in Sri Lanka

Biological & Field Challenges:

  • Foliar Disease Epidemics: Bacterial Blight, Leaf Blast, and Brown Spot destroy between 20% and 80% of paddy harvest if undetected at early tillering stages.
  • Pest Infestation Spread: Brown planthoppers, stem borers, and leaf folders cause 20% to 40% yield loss and transmit viral dwarf diseases.
  • Extension Officer Shortage: Rural farming districts have few agricultural officers, causing diagnostic consultation delays of days or weeks.
  • Agrochemical Misuse: Trial-and-error spraying of uncalibrated pesticides increases costs, harms beneficial predators, and contaminates water cascades.

Technological & AI Limitations:

  • Unrealistic Closed-World Assumption: Deployed AI systems predict unknown weeds or insects as target diseases with dangerously high confidence.
  • Digital Divide & Low Literacy: Smallholder farmers struggle with English mobile forms and technical botanical terminology.
  • Lack of Multimodal Fusion: No single platform integrates speech symptoms, leaf pathology, pest vision, and verified chemical advisory.
  • Hallucination Risks: Generative AI without deterministic expert rules produces hazardous chemical recommendations.
Section 4

Research Objectives

Main objective and specific objectives allocated equally (25% each) across the 4 modules

Main Research Objective

To design, develop, and evaluate PaddyGuard AI: a multimodal intelligent decision support system that integrates deep learning foliar disease classification, field pest detection, Sinhala voice-based symptom analysis, Out-of-Distribution (OOD) reliability screening, and a knowledge-driven treatment advisory chatbot with calibrated dosage calculations.

Component 1 (25% Weight)

Voice-Based Symptom Diagnosis

  • Collect and curate a dedicated Sinhala spoken symptom description corpus from rural farmer interviews.
  • Implement and evaluate speech-to-text transcription using Whisper and Vosk models.
  • Extract clinical symptom entities via TF-IDF and compare BERT text classification against traditional ML baselines.
  • Synthesize diagnostic summaries and guidance into natural Sinhala audio (TTS) for low-literacy users.
Component 2 (25% Weight)

Rice Leaf Disease Classification + OOD

  • Curate a balanced 4-class foliar dataset (Bacterial Blight, Leaf Blast, Brown Spot, Healthy) with non-rice OOD probe sets.
  • Benchmark transfer-learning CNN backbones (ResNet50, EfficientNetB3, DenseNet121) targeting > 92% accuracy.
  • Calibrate ODIN, Mahalanobis Distance, and OpenMax to achieve an OOD rejection rate > 85% at FPR95.
  • Generate Grad-CAM heatmaps to visually verify that predictions attend to biologically meaningful lesion regions.
Component 4 (25% Weight)

Rice Pest Detection + OOD

  • Curate representative paddy insect datasets from IP02 and agricultural repositories.
  • Train and evaluate deep detection models (YOLOv8, ResNet50) for Brown Planthopper, Stem Borer, Leaf Folder, Gall Midge.
  • Implement OOD detection mechanisms to identify and reject unknown insects not belonging to the paddy pest taxonomy.
  • Benchmark precision, recall, and detection latency under complex field foliage backgrounds.
Component 3 (25% Weight)

Treatment Advisory & RAG Chatbot

  • Construct a vectorized agricultural knowledge repository from IRRI Rice Knowledge Bank, FAO, and DOA Sri Lanka guidelines.
  • Implement semantic similarity retrieval using FAISS/ChromaDB paired with LLM response generation.
  • Formulate deterministic expert rules to strictly validate and verify chemical and biological treatment options.
  • Develop a calibrated land acreage-to-dosage sprayer calculator to prevent agrochemical misuse.
Section 5

Research Methodology

Design Science Research (DSR) lifecycle guiding iterative artifact development

This research adopts the Design Science Research (DSR) paradigm (Hevner et al. [13]), which focuses on designing and evaluating innovative technological artifacts to solve practical human problems. The methodology progresses through iterative design, implementation, empirical testing, and refinement cycles across all four research components:

Phase 1: Foundations

Literature analysis, problem framing, multi-source dataset curation (Kaggle benchmarks, farmer speech recordings, local field photography).

Phase 2: Model Development

CNN leaf transfer learning, YOLOv8 pest training, Whisper ASR phonetic tuning, and FAISS vector knowledge engineering.

Phase 3: Integration & Evaluation

OOD threshold calibration (ODIN/OpenMax), Grad-CAM expert review, deterministic rule validation, and latency profiling under mobile networks.
